# 16 University GTM playbook (scripts + speeches)

> Founder-facing playbook for landing university placement cells as the
> supply-and-trust channel for Stuviora. Pairs with `gtm.md` (overall
> GTM) and the verified GTM research in `full-build-plan.md` S7.5
> (liquidity-first, beachhead, trust in the MVP). No em-dashes or
> en-dashes, so any line here can be lifted straight into outreach copy.

## 0. The one idea to hold in your head

You are not selling software to the university. **It is free to them.**
You are giving a placement cell a turnkey way to deliver three things
they are already under pressure to deliver, at zero cost and zero risk:

1. **Paid, real-world work** for their students (income + a portfolio).
2. **Experiential-learning and skilling proof** for NEP 2020, AICTE,
   and NAAC/NIRF accreditation narratives.
3. **A clean dashboard** they can show their Dean and management,
   without lifting a finger to run it.

Everything in the scripts below ladders back to those three. The student
is the user; the placement cell is the distribution channel; the SMB
client is who pays. Keep the roles straight.

## 1. Why universities are the wedge (the strategy)

- **Cold-start needs supply density in one place.** A marketplace dies
  without liquidity. A single college WhatsApp group of 300 motivated
  students is denser, cheaper supply than a national ad spend.
- **Trust travels through the institution.** "Your placement cell
  endorsed this" beats any ad. Verified .ac.in emails plus the AI
  quality gate plus escrow make us safe to endorse.
- **It is a beachhead, not a broadcast.** Win 1 college fully (high
  activation, real payouts, happy students) before touching the next.
  A proof college becomes the reference for the next ten.

## 2. Who to target, in order

### Tier-A first colleges (pick 3, go deep)
Choose for *density and reachability*, not prestige:
- A placement cell or faculty coordinator who will actually pick up the
  phone (warm intro beats cold every time).
- 200+ students in fields with clear freelance demand: content/English,
  design/fine arts, commerce/BBA, CS/IT, data, marketing.
- An active student culture (clubs, an entrepreneurship cell, a coding
  or design society).

### The stakeholder map (who actually says yes)
| Role | What they care about | Your angle |
|---|---|---|
| Placement / T&P Officer | Placement %, employability, reporting, low effort | Free income + portfolio for students; dashboard; zero work |
| Faculty coordinator / club lead | Student engagement, real projects | A live brief pipeline students can earn from |
| HoD / Dean | Accreditation (NAAC/NIRF), NEP/AICTE compliance | Documented experiential learning + skilling outcomes |
| Entrepreneurship / Incubation cell | Student entrepreneurship, real revenue | Students running micro-businesses on day one |
| Students (the real user) | Money, experience, safety, "is this legit" | Verified, escrow-protected, AI-checked, 85% to you |

Start with whoever will meet you fastest. Often it is a single
enthusiastic faculty coordinator or a student club lead, not the
official T&P office. Use them to get the official meeting.

## 3. The outreach sequence (cold to expansion)

```
Warm intro / cold email  ->  15-min discovery call  ->  30-min pitch to
placement cell  ->  free pilot (1 cohort, 30 to 50 students, 4 weeks)
->  results review  ->  formal endorsement + ambassador  ->  next dept
->  next college (reference-led)
```

Rule: never ask for a contract first. Ask for a **pilot**. The pilot is
free, time-boxed, and reversible, which removes every reason to say no.

## 4. SCRIPTS

### 4.1 Cold email to a placement officer (short, specific, no ask for money)

> **Subject:** A free way to get [College] students paid freelance work
>
> Dear [Name],
>
> I run Stuviora, an India-first platform where verified college students
> earn from real freelance projects (content, design, development, data)
> for small businesses. Every delivery is checked by our AI quality gate
> before the client sees it, and payments are escrow-protected, so it is
> safe for both sides.
>
> It is free for [College] and free for your students. They keep 85% of
> every project. We handle payments, quality, and disputes.
>
> I would love to run a small, no-cost pilot with one batch of your
> students and give your office a simple dashboard of their activity and
> earnings, which also documents experiential learning for NAAC and NEP.
>
> Could I have 15 minutes this week to show you how it works? I can call
> at a time that suits you.
>
> Warm regards,
> [Your name] | Stuviora | [phone] | stuviora.com
> Hire students. Trust the platform.

Keep it under 150 words. One link. One ask (15 minutes). Send Tuesday to
Thursday morning. Follow up twice, 3 and 7 days later, each shorter.

### 4.2 LinkedIn / WhatsApp opener (warm or semi-warm)

> Hi [Name], I saw you lead [placement / the coding society] at
> [College]. I am building Stuviora, where verified students earn from
> real freelance projects with AI-checked quality and escrow-protected
> pay. It is free for the college and students keep 85%. Open to a quick
> 15-minute look? I think your students would genuinely benefit.

### 4.3 Cold-call / discovery-call script (15 minutes)

**Open (15 seconds):**
> "Hi [Name], this is [you] from Stuviora. Do you have two minutes? I
> work with placement cells to get students paid freelance work safely,
> at no cost to the college. Is now an okay time, or should I call back?"

**Hook (30 seconds):**
> "Quick version: students sign up with their college email, so they are
> verified. Small businesses post real projects. Students bid, do the
> work, and before any client sees the delivery, our AI checks it against
> the brief and scores it. Payment sits in escrow and releases on
> approval. Students keep 85%. It is free for you and for them."

**Discover (the half you should spend most time on, ask and listen):**
> - "How do students today get real-world experience or internships?"
> - "What does your office report on for NAAC or NIRF around skilling
>   and experiential learning?"
> - "If students could earn a few thousand rupees a month doing real
>   work, with you able to see it all on a dashboard, would that be
>   useful to your placement story?"

**Close to a meeting (not to a yes):**
> "I do not want to pitch you cold. Can I get 30 minutes with you and
> maybe one faculty coordinator next week to show the actual product and
> propose a small free pilot? Tuesday or Thursday better?"

Use this in the 30-minute meeting. Speak it, do not read it.

> "Thank you for the time. Let me be direct about what this is and what
> it is not.
>
> Stuviora is a platform where your students earn money doing real
> freelance projects for small businesses. Writing, design, development,
> data work, marketing. It is completely free for the college and for
> the students. Students keep 85% of what they earn.
>
> Three things make it safe enough for you to put your name behind it.
>

> know they are real, enrolled students.
>

> our AI reviews it against the original brief and scores it from 0 to

> talent. Quality is protected on both sides.
>
> Three, money is held in escrow and released only on approval, with a
> built-in dispute process. No student gets cheated, no client loses

>

> kinds of projects they are doing. That is documented experiential
> learning and skilling, which maps directly to NEP 2020 and what NAAC

> students from this college earned X rupees doing real work this term.
>
> What I am asking for is not a contract. I am asking to run one free
> pilot with a single batch of 30 to 50 students for four weeks. We
> onboard them, we get them their first projects, and at the end I come
> back and show you exactly what happened. If it does not work, you have
> lost nothing. If it does, we expand to more departments together.
>
> Can we pick a batch to start with?"

### 4.5 Objection handling (memorize these)

- **"Is this legitimate / safe?"**
  > "Fair question. Three guardrails: college-email verification, an AI
  > quality check on every delivery, and escrow so money only moves on
  > approval. Plus a dispute process. I can show you a live example
  > right now."

- **"Will it distract students from studies?"**
  > "It is project-based and fully student-controlled. They take work
  > when they have time, often aligned with what they are already
  > learning. Most do one or two small projects a month. It builds the
  > portfolio that gets them hired later."

- **"What does it cost us?"**
  > "Nothing. No fee, no setup, no minimum. We earn a small commission
  > only when a student successfully completes a paid project, and the
  > student still keeps 85%. If students do not earn, we do not earn."

- **"We already have an internship/placement program."**
  > "This complements it. Internships are a few seats; this is paid work
  > available to every student, all year, with a portfolio outcome you
  > can report. It strengthens your placement story rather than
  > replacing anything."

- **"How is this different from Internshala / Upwork / Fiverr?"**
  > "Two things. One, we are India-first and student-only, so the work
  > and the trust are built for your students. Two, the inline AI quality
  > gate. On other platforms a student competes unprotected against
  > professionals and a client gambles. Here, quality is checked before
  > it reaches the client, which is what makes students hireable from day
  > one."

- **"Data privacy?"**
  > "We follow India's DPDP Act. Students consent to what we process,
  > they can download or delete their data, and the placement-cell
  > dashboard shows aggregate cohort numbers, not sensitive personal
  > details."

- **"Send me an email / let me think."**
  > "Of course, I will send a one-pager today. To make it concrete, can
  > we pencil a date to start a free pilot, even tentatively? If you
  > change your mind, we cancel, no cost. What does the calendar look
  > like for the next two weeks?"

## 5. The pilot design (what "start our work" means)

A pilot that proves the model in 4 weeks:
- **Scope:** one department or club, 30 to 50 students.
- **Week 1:** townhall + onboarding drive. Target 40%+ signup, each with
  one skill and one portfolio sample.
- **Week 2:** seed demand. You (the founder) hand-source 5 to 10 real
  briefs from your own SMB network so students get matched fast. Do not
  wait for organic demand in a pilot; manufacture the first wins.
- **Week 3 to 4:** first deliveries pass the AI gate, first payouts land.
  Capture 2 to 3 student testimonials and one screenshot of a payout.
- **Review:** bring the placement cell the dashboard numbers, the
  testimonials, and one "money win" story. Ask for the endorsement and
  the next department.

**Definition of pilot success (state it up front so the bar is shared):**
- >= 40% of the cohort signs up.
- >= 10 students complete at least one paid project.
- >= 1 quotable testimonial and 1 payout screenshot.
- The placement cell agrees to expand to another department.

## 6. Land and expand (after one college works)

1. **Turn the pilot into a reference.** A one-page case study: students
   onboarded, projects completed, total earned, a quote from the
   placement officer. This is your single most powerful sales asset.
2. **Expand within the college** department by department before leaving.
3. **Reference-led outreach to the next college:** "We ran a pilot with
   [College A]'s placement cell. Their students earned X in four weeks.
   I would like to do the same for [College B]." Warm, proven, low-risk.
4. **Formalize with the keenest colleges** via the partner dashboard and
   the signed B2B API (cohort GMV and activation reporting), so the
   placement cell self-serves its numbers.
5. **Ambassadors compound it.** Each campus runs its own monthly session;
   you move from doing every townhall to enabling them.

## 7. Materials you need (build these once)

- A one-page PDF for placement cells (the three benefits, the three
  safety guardrails, the pilot offer, your contact).
- A live demo logged in as a student (show the AI gate scoring a real
  delivery; this is the moment that sells).
- A QR-code sign-up poster and a slide deck (8 slides max).
- A short payout-win reel (a real student, a real number) for the
  townhall and for socials.
- The placement-cell dashboard view (cohort signups, active students,
  total earned).
- A consent and data one-pager (DPDP) for cautious administrators.

## 8. Metrics to run the motion by

- **Outreach:** emails sent -> calls booked -> meetings held -> pilots
  started (track conversion at each step; fix the worst step first).
- **Pilot:** signup rate, first-project rate, time to first payout,
  testimonials captured.
- **Expansion:** colleges live, departments per college, ambassador
  count, reference conversions.

The north star for this channel is not colleges signed. It is
**active, earning students per college.** A logo with no liquidity is
vanity. One college where 100 students are earning is the whole thesis
proven.

## 9. Worst-case and hostile questions (crisis-grade)

The questions in 4.5 are the warm-up. These are the ones a cautious
administrator, a skeptical committee member, or a worried parent asks
when they are looking for a reason to say no. Fumble one and the deal
dies, so rehearse these until they are reflex.

### How to answer a hostile room (the four rules)
1. **Acknowledge the concern as legitimate first.** Never get defensive.
   "That is a fair question" buys you the room.
2. **Answer with a specific guardrail, not a promise.** "Escrow holds
   the money upfront" beats "do not worry, students get paid."
3. **Where real risk exists, show the mitigation and the escalation
   path. Do not deny it.** Honesty is more credible than a perfect story.
4. **Keep the risk on us, never on the college.** The pilot, the escrow,
   the endorse-only-after-proof structure all exist so they commit
   nothing until the numbers are in front of them.

### Student safety and harm

- **"What if a student does the work and the client refuses to pay or
  disappears?"**
  > "That cannot happen the way it does off-platform. The client funds
  > escrow upfront, before the student starts, so the money is already
  > locked. On approval it releases. If the client goes silent after a
  > passing delivery, it auto-releases to the student in 72 hours. The
  > student is paid whether or not the client responds. That is the core
  > reason we exist."

- **"What if a client harasses a student, especially a female student?"**
  > "Safety is non-negotiable for us, same as for you. Contact details
  > stay hidden until a project is active, all messaging happens on the
  > platform so there is a record, and any student can report a client in
  > one tap. We suspend a reported client immediately and block them from
  > that student while we investigate. I will walk your cell through the
  > exact escalation path so you always know who to call."

- **"What happens to a student mid-project if there is a dispute?"**
  > "The money stays locked in escrow, untouched, while both sides submit
  > evidence and we review it against the brief and the AI score. Neither
  > side can grab the funds. The student is never left having worked for
  > nothing while it is sorted out."

### Reputation and liability for the college

- **"What if the AI approves weak work and our students look incompetent?
  It reflects on us."**
  > "The AI gate exists precisely to prevent that. It scores every
  > delivery against the brief before the client sees it and sends fixes
  > back to the student first, so weak work gets improved, not shipped. It
  > is not magic, which is why escrow, revisions, and a dispute process
  > sit behind it. The whole system is built so a student's first
  > impression is a good one."

- **"If a student plagiarizes or fakes the work and a client sues, are we
  liable?"**
  > "Two parts. First, liability: the college is not a party to any
  > project. The agreement is between the student and the client on our
  > platform. You are the introducer, not a guarantor, and none of the
  > legal risk sits with your name. Second, our review flags obvious
  > issues before delivery, and anything contested goes through our
  > dispute process with the funds held in escrow until it is resolved."

- **"What if this embarrasses our placement cell? Management will ask why
  we backed an unknown startup."**
  > "That is exactly why we start with a small, private pilot and a shared
  > definition of success before any name goes public. You endorse it only
  > after you have watched real students earn real money on your own
  > dashboard. The reputational risk is on me to earn the endorsement. You
  > commit nothing until the numbers are in front of you."

- **"A parent called, worried their child is being scammed or
  distracted."**
  > "Completely understandable, and I will give you a one-page note to
  > share with parents. The short version: it is free, the student is paid
  > through protected escrow so they cannot be cheated, the work is
  > project-based and on their own schedule, and it builds the portfolio
  > that helps them get placed. I would rather a worried parent see the
  > guardrails than guess at them."

### Trust in us as a company

- **"You are a startup. What if you shut down? Our students lose money and
  data."**
  > "Fair, we are early-stage and I will not pretend otherwise. Two
  > protections. Money sits in escrow ring-fenced with our payment partner
  > Razorpay. It is not cash we hold or could run off with, and in-flight
  > projects settle to the rule. Data: under India's DPDP law every
  > student can download or delete their data any time, so their portfolio
  > and records are portable, never trapped. I would rather earn your
  > trust with a small pilot than ask you to bet the college on us."

- **"What is the catch? How do you really make money? Is this cheap
  student labor?"**
  > "No catch and no hidden fee. We take 15% only when a student
  > successfully completes a paid project, and the student keeps 85%. If
  > they do not earn, we do not earn, so we are aligned with them making
  > money. It is the opposite of cheap labor: the AI gate and ratings push
  > a student's rates up over time as they build a track record, and they
  > keep the large majority of every rupee."

- **"How do we know you will not sell our students' data?"**
  > "We cannot, lawfully. Under DPDP we may only use data for the
  > purposes a student consents to, students can withdraw consent and
  > delete their data any time, and the dashboard we give you shows
  > aggregate cohort numbers, not sensitive personal details. Selling data
  > would be both illegal and suicidal for a platform whose entire value
  > is trust."

- **"Why should we trust you over Internshala or an established name?"**
  > "Two reasons. We are built only for students, so the work and the
  > trust fit them rather than throwing them against professionals. And
  > the inline AI quality gate is something no incumbent has. It is what
  > makes a first-time student safe to hire and safe for you to endorse.
  > I will prove it with a free pilot rather than ask you to take it on
  > brand."

### Legal, tax, and compliance

- **"Are we turning our students into tax defaulters? GST? TDS?"**
  > "For the amounts students earn, almost none of this applies. Freelance
  > services are exempt from GST registration below 20 lakh a year, and
  > platform TDS only begins above 5 lakh a year per student, far above
  > what a typical student earns. For a rare high earner we deduct and
  > report correctly under Section 194-O and give them their tax records.
  > Students stay compliant by default, and we show a simple tax summary
  > in their account."

- **"Are these students employees? Any labor-law obligation on us or
  you?"**
  > "No. Students are independent freelancers choosing project-based work.
  > There is no employment relationship with the college or with us. The
  > college is the introducer, not an employer. It is the same legal
  > footing as any freelance marketplace."

- **"What if a client posts inappropriate or fake jobs?"**
  > "Clients are screened, listings are visible to us, and students can
  > report a job in one tap. We remove bad listings and suspend bad
  > clients. And because payment is escrow-first, a fake job cannot
  > extract free work in the first place."

### Practical failure

- **"We tried something like this before and it flopped. Engagement
  died."**
  > "Most do, and almost always for one reason: no projects, so students
  > sign up and nothing happens. That is exactly what the pilot is built
  > to solve. In the first weeks I personally bring real briefs from my
  > own network so students get matched and paid fast. We measure success
  > as earning students, not signups. If we cannot get real money into
  > students' hands in four weeks, you should not expand, and that is the
  > deal I am offering."

### The one-line fallbacks (when you are cornered)
- On safety: "Escrow holds the money before any work starts. The student
  cannot be cheated."
- On quality: "Nothing reaches a client until the AI checks it against
  the brief."
- On risk to the college: "You endorse nothing until you have seen real
  students earning on your own dashboard."
- On us: "We only make money when your students do."
